02 — Conversion Tracking

If a conversion isn't firing, I find out why

A page isn't done until its leads show up in GA4 and Google Ads. This is the tracking setup I use on every launch.

  1. Plan the events firstList each action that matters, such as form submit, call click, booking or download, and name it the same way across GA4 and Ads.
  2. Fire tags from the dataLayerPush events on real success, not on button clicks, so a failed form never counts as a lead.
  3. Map to GA4 and Google AdsSend GA4 events with parameters, link GA4 key events to Ads, and add Ads conversion tags with Conversion Linker.
  4. Verify before launchCheck every conversion in Tag Assistant preview and GA4 DebugView, then confirm it in Google Ads diagnostics.
  5. Watch after launchCompare conversions to real leads each week. A sudden drop to zero usually means a plugin update or form change broke something.

03 — Launch QA

What "done" means before a client sees it

I run this checklist on every landing page, and I'd use the same one when reviewing a junior developer's work.

Design

  • Matches the mockup in spacing, alignment and type
  • Checked at 360, 768, 1024 and 1440px widths
  • Real devices: iPhone Safari and Android Chrome
  • No layout shift when fonts and images load

Forms & CTAs

  • Test submission received by the client inbox or CRM
  • Validation and error states work
  • tel: links and booking links work on mobile
  • Thank-you state or page shows correctly

Tracking

  • GA4 event visible in DebugView
  • Google Ads conversion recorded
  • No duplicate fires on refresh
  • UTM and gclid preserved through the form

Performance & SEO

  • PageSpeed and Core Web Vitals checked on mobile
  • Images compressed and sized
  • Title, meta, one H1, and noindex where needed
  • Staging to production with no broken URLs
Why tracking is on the checklist

A page can look right and send its emails while Google Ads records zero conversions. A form plugin that redirects before the tag fires, or a plugin update that renames a form, is enough to cause it. That's why I don't call a page done until I've seen the conversion appear in Google Ads.
